Arguments surrounding voter turnout focus on civic responsibility, impact, and systemic disillusionment. Proponents argue that voting is a fundamental civic duty essential for holding elected officials accountable, ensuring diverse representation, influencing policies on issues like healthcare and education, and protecting democratic rights. Conversely, those who advocate for abstention or choose not to vote argue that withholding a vote is a valid political statement against unappealing candidates or a flawed system, noting that individual votes carry minimal statistical weight, uninformed voting may yield poor outcomes, and certain religious or ideological beliefs discourage participation in government altogether.
