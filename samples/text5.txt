The discussion about voter turnout centers on three main aspects which include civic responsibility and the effects of voting and the general public's loss of faith in the system. Supporters of voting believe it serves as a basic civic obligation which enables citizens to monitor their elected representatives while preserving democratic values and directing government decisions about healthcare and education and protecting democratic rights. The supporters of abstention and non-voting choose to withhold their votes because they see this action as an effective way to express their opposition to both undesirable political candidates and the current dysfunctional electoral system. People who oppose voting choose to withhold their votes because they believe this action makes an effective political statement against both unappealing candidates and the existing broken system. The statistical value of individual votes becomes negligible when people vote without proper knowledge which results in negative election results. Some religious and ideological beliefs actively prevent their followers from taking part in government activities.
